After the rains clear out in late October, Chiang Mai slips into its best stretch of the year. The humidity drops, the skies go a deep clean blue, and the hills — green from six months of rain — are still lush before the dry season browns them off. Mornings are genuinely cool, especially up the mountains, and the air is clear before the burning season haze arrives in the new year.

🏵️The festival of lights: Yi Peng & Loy Krathong

November is when Chiang Mai lights up — literally. Two festivals overlap around the November full moon: Loy Krathong, when people float little flower-and-candle rafts (krathong) down the Ping River, and Yi Peng, the Lanna tradition of releasing glowing paper sky lanterns (khom loi) into the night.

The dates move each year with the lunar calendar, so they don't land on the same days every November. Around the city, the Ping River, Tha Phae Gate, and the Old City temples are the heart of it. Some large lantern-release events are ticketed and sell out months ahead.

Good to know

Floating lanterns are restricted near the airport on safety grounds, and flight schedules shift around the festival — check your dates. If you want the big mass-release experience, book the ticketed event early; the free riverside releases are more spontaneous and just as lovely.

⛰️Go up the mountains

This is the month the mountains pay off. Doi Suthep is an easy half-day — the golden chedi of Wat Phra That is a short ride up, and clear November air means you can actually see the city laid out below. For the full day, Doi Inthanon — Thailand's highest peak — is cold enough at the summit for a jacket in the morning, with the twin royal pagodas, the Kew Mae Pan nature trail, and a waterfall on the way down.

If you'd rather not drive the switchbacks yourself, this is the time to let a local guide handle it and just enjoy the view.

💦Still good: the warm-weather things

You don't have to give up the water just because it's cooler. Bamboo rafting on the rivers north of the city still runs through the day — the water's a touch cooler than in the hot season, so go midday when the sun is up if you feel the chill. Bua Tong "sticky" waterfall, the no-riding elephant camps in the Mae Taeng valley, and the coffee farms in the hills are all at their best with the trails dry and the greenery still in.

☕Eat & drink

Cool evenings make November the perfect month for the night markets — you can actually sit and eat without melting. It's peak khao soi weather, and the Sunday Walking Street is at its liveliest. For something quieter, warm up with a pot of local highland tea — Monsoon Tea grows theirs in the forests of Northern Thailand and has a calm teahouse in town, a nice counterpoint to a cold mountain morning.

🎒Before you go

  • Pack a light layer. It's shorts-and-t-shirt by day, but mornings and mountain tops genuinely need a fleece or jacket.
  • Book ahead. November is peak season, and festival week especially — rooms, tours, and the ticketed lantern events fill up early.
  • Float responsibly. If you make a krathong, choose the biodegradable banana-leaf kind over styrofoam — the river thanks you.
